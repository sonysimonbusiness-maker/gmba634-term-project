# 1. Ports: container volume moved away from the West Coast

**Claim.** Container volume at West Coast ports fell 32% from 2021 to 2023 (20.2 million to 13.7 million TEUs), more than the Atlantic Coast (down 15%) or the Gulf Coast (down 7%).

![Line chart of container volume at 25 US ports by coast, 2016 to 2023. The Pacific Coast line rises to 20.2 million TEUs in 2021 and falls to 13.7 million in 2023. The Atlantic line peaks at 20.3 million in 2021 and falls to 17.2 million. The Gulf line stays near 4.5 million.](chart.png)

## Story boxes (DRAFT, to be reviewed in my own words)
1. **Ask.** I am asking a freight company's capacity planner to plan more capacity at Gulf and Atlantic ports than at West Coast ports in the next planning round.
2. **Situation.** Container volume at the 25 US ports that report every year rose from 35.9 million TEUs in 2016 to a peak of 45.3 million in 2021.
3. **Complication.** By 2023 volume was back to 35.4 million. The Pacific Coast fell 32% in two years, the Atlantic Coast 15%, the Gulf Coast 7%. Houston grew 76% and Savannah 31% since 2016; Los Angeles and Long Beach fell about 5%.
4. **Question.** So should we plan more capacity at Gulf and Atlantic ports than at West Coast ports?
5. **Resolution.** Shift planning toward Gulf and Atlantic ports. Owner: the capacity planner. Date: UNKNOWN. Cost: UNKNOWN. Rejected option: keep the capacity split as it is.
6. **Reversal.** I would reverse this if the next data release showed Pacific Coast volume back near its 2021 level of 20.2 million TEUs. I would look in the same BTS port data.

## Data note
Source: US DOT Bureau of Transportation Statistics, Port Data (https://data.bts.gov/d/5rpz-kgm9). Public Domain. Filter: cargo type CONTAINER, trade type TOTAL, units Container TEUs, and only the 25 ports that report in all 8 years (2016 to 2023), because the number of reporting ports changes by year. `data.csv` holds the three regional totals.
